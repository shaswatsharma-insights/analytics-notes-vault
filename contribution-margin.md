# Contribution Margin

> Contribution margin tells you how much each sale contributes toward covering fixed costs and generating profit.

---

## 1. What is Contribution Margin?

Contribution Margin (CM) is the amount of revenue left after subtracting **variable costs**.

It shows how much money each unit contributes to:

- covering fixed costs  
- and then generating profit  

### Formula (per unit)

Contribution Margin = Selling Price − Variable Cost

### Formula (total)

Total Contribution Margin = Total Revenue − Total Variable Costs

---

## 2. Why This Concept Matters

Revenue alone is misleading.

Profit can also be misleading in the short run.

Contribution margin answers a more practical question:

> If we sell one more unit, does it help the business?

If contribution margin is positive:
- Each additional unit helps cover fixed costs.
- After fixed costs are covered, it creates profit.

If contribution margin is negative:
- Every additional sale makes the situation worse.

This is critical in pricing, scaling, and short-term decisions.

---

## 3. Where It Shows Up in Business Decisions

### Pricing Decisions
Before lowering price, you must check:
Will contribution margin remain positive?

### Break-even Analysis
Break-even happens when:

Total Contribution Margin = Total Fixed Costs

Only after that point does the firm earn profit.

### Scaling Decisions
High revenue growth with low contribution margin can destroy a business.
Strong contribution margin allows safe scaling.

### Product Mix Decisions
When resources are limited, businesses prioritize products with higher contribution margin per unit of constraint.

---

## 4. Common Misunderstandings

### 1. High revenue means healthy business
False.
If variable costs are high, contribution margin may be weak.

### 2. Profit is enough to judge performance
Profit includes fixed costs, accounting choices, and timing effects.
Contribution margin isolates operational strength.

### 3. “Fixed costs don’t matter”
They matter — but contribution margin tells you whether you are moving toward covering them.

---

## 5. Decision Intuition

Contribution margin is about marginal thinking.

It asks:
What happens if we sell one more unit?

This makes it one of the most important concepts in:
- pricing strategy  
- unit economics  
- startup models  
- cost control  
- short-run decision making  

If contribution margin is strong, the business has operating leverage.
If it is weak, growth alone will not save it.

---

## Final Clarity

Revenue shows activity.

Contribution margin shows operational health.

Profit shows final outcome.

Understanding the difference prevents poor decisions.
