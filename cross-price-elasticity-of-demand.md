# Cross-Price Elasticity of Demand

## 1. What is this concept?

Cross-price elasticity of demand measures how the quantity demanded of one good changes when the price of another good changes.

It answers:

“If the price of Good B changes, what happens to demand for Good A?”

Formula:

Cross-price elasticity (XED)  
= (% change in quantity demanded of Good A)  
  / (% change in price of Good B)

It is always about two different goods.

---

## 2. How to interpret the sign

The sign is more important than the number at first.

### Positive Cross-Price Elasticity  
If XED > 0  

The goods are **substitutes**.

Example:  
If the price of coffee increases and demand for tea increases,  
tea and coffee are substitutes.

People switch.

---

### Negative Cross-Price Elasticity  
If XED < 0  

The goods are **complements**.

Example:  
If the price of petrol increases and demand for cars decreases,  
cars and petrol are complements.

They are used together.

---

### Zero or Near Zero  
If XED ≈ 0  

The goods are unrelated.

Example:  
Price of laptops changing does not meaningfully affect demand for bananas.

---

## 3. Why this matters in decisions

Cross-price elasticity helps answer strategic questions like:

- Who are my real competitors?
- Do customers see alternatives?
- What happens if a competitor raises prices?
- Should I bundle products?

If a substitute raises prices, your demand may increase.

If a complement raises prices, your demand may fall.

This affects:
- Pricing strategy
- Revenue forecasting
- Competitive positioning
- Bundling decisions
- Partnership strategy

---

## 4. Where this shows up in analytics / business

### Pricing Models
Understanding how competitor price changes affect your sales.

### Demand Forecasting
Estimating second-order effects when industry prices shift.

### Platform Economics
Complement effects between hardware and software.

### Retail & FMCG
Brand substitution behavior when one SKU price changes.

---

## 5. Important distinctions

### Substitutes vs Competitors

Not all competitors are strong substitutes.

If XED is small but positive, switching exists but is weak.

Real competition is measured, not assumed.

---

### Complement strength matters

Strong complements:
- Printer and ink

Weak complements:
- Burger and cola

The more tightly connected the usage, the stronger the negative elasticity.

---

## 6. Common misunderstandings

❌ It does not measure how sensitive demand is to its own price.  
That is price elasticity of demand.

❌ A positive value does not mean “good”.  
It simply describes substitution.

❌ It is not symmetric in real life.  
The effect of B on A may differ from A on B due to scale and market size.

---

## 7. Simple intuition summary

Cross-price elasticity tells you:

Do customers switch?
Or do they consume together?

It measures relationships between products.

Understanding those relationships is strategic power.
